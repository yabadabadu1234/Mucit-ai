Today I will speak about planned obsolescence and its legal, rational and moral ruling.
I will not argue from objects. A specific mechanism, an interchangeable component, an energy storage unit or an executable routine carries its own physics, its own safety rules and its own statutory standards, and the moment I name one of them, the argument turns into a debate about that specific artifact and I lose the universal principle. So I will abstract the principle away from every specific physical manifestation. In the language of the jurists this is the purification of the operative legal ground, tenkîh-i menât: I remove the brand, the product category, and the specific material substrate, and I retain only that upon which the legal ruling truly depends. What remains is a model defined by economic architecture, cyber-physical ecosystems, strict delictual liability, and objective normative variables. Every argument in this presentation is built exclusively upon this model and its four archetypes.

Here are the variables. 
The maker or platform architect is M (al-Sâni'). The retail vendor or intermediate distributor who transfers custody is S (al-Bâi'). The end-user buyer or owner is B (al-Mushtarî). 
The durable cyber-physical asset is X. The acquisition price paid by B is P. 
The baseline structural durability and functional capacity benchmark of X is E: measured in authentic units of operational capacity and fatigue resistance. Crucially, E is not an imaginary, uncontracted laboratory maximum, nor an infinite ideal exceeding the product's price tier. E represents the authentic, unadulterated engineering capacity and fatigue resistance inherent in the composite bill of materials, structural chassis, and primary subsystems chosen by M. 
The artificial truncation, modular lockout, or intentional bottlenecking engineered by M is L, representing a premature operational limit strictly inferior to E. Crucially, L is not the natural, physical wear of an internal wear-bound subcomponent—which obeys the inescapable physical laws of material entropy—but M's affirmative design or algorithmic choice to deliberately obstruct the non-destructive renewal, uncoupled replacement, or logical re-calibration of that subcomponent. L is the deliberate structural tethering that forces the premature abandonment of the entire asset X while its primary functional assemblies retain unexhausted operational capacity.
The technical and proportional necessity filter is T: governed by systemic proportionality (al-tenâsüb) and the prohibition of the abuse of rights (sû-i isti'mâl-i hak, Mecelle Article 22), where a marginal engineering trade-off or ancillary safety constraint cannot justify the catastrophic destruction of core asset capacity (E - L).
The captive switching barrier and ecosystem dependency is W (Captive Lock-in): representing the cumulative structural, contractual, physical-interoperability, or capital barrier that prevents B from substituting M's platform or accessing independent maintenance. Where W is high, B is deprived of effective market exit and coerced into captive repeat transactions.
And the public ecological and systemic commons threshold is C: the non-negotiable boundary of public interest (maslahah 'âmmah / darar âmm) that invalidates private contractual destruction of the planetary carrying capacity and scarce thermodynamic resources regardless of individual buyer assent.

THE ROOT CHARACTERIZATION: THE SIX JURISTIC HARMONIES.
Before examining the models, the legal and economic reality of planned obsolescence must be freed from six critical misconceptions:

First, The Normative Definition of Defect versus Natural Fatigue Gradient (Ayıp ile Doğal Mukavemet Gradyanı Ayrımı):
In reliability engineering and materials science, a complex multi-component cyber-physical asset inevitably incorporates constituent materials possessing divergent rates of natural degradation and physical entropy (e.g., solid-state semiconductors endure far longer than chemical storage media or mechanical contact interfaces). Classical jurisprudence recognizes that the measure of defect (ayb) in an asset is not determined by comparing the lifespan of a wear-bound subcomponent against the enduring longevity of the structural chassis (which would constitute kıyâs-ı maalfârık). Rather, the actionable wrong and latent defect (ayb-i hafî / taghrîr) resides strictly in the deliberate structural or algorithmic immobilization of non-destructive modular renewal: namely, where M intentionally designs the asset as an inseparable monolithic monolith, or deploys proprietary lockouts, so that the natural exhaustion of an inexpensive, wear-bound auxiliary subcomponent forcibly precipitates the constructive destruction (helâk-ı hükmî) and total abandonment of the enduring primary asset. The crime is not that parts wear out at differing speeds; the crime is taking the durable chassis hostage to the premature death of a single locked subcomponent.

Second, Resolving the Lemons Paradox: Dynamic Opacity versus Market Equilibrium (Limon Piyasası ile Gabn Geriliminin Çözümü):
An apparent tension arises between Evidence 1 (B pays full price P expecting durability E) and Evidence 8 (the market degrades into a lemon equilibrium where all goods fail at L and prices adjust). This tension dissolves once the market is understood dynamically rather than statically:
At the micro-contract level (Evidence 1), transactions occur under informational opacity (taghrîr) or within platform-locked ecosystems (W), where M extracts a price P reflecting customary durability E while secretly delivering L.
At the macro-market level (Evidence 8), the lemons dynamic operates as a destructive temporal vector: prolonged deceit systematically drives durable producers out of the market until honesty becomes economically impossible. In platform oligopolies, moreover, switching costs W permanently sever price P from competitive discipline, allowing M to charge premium prices for truncated lifespans even when consumers suspect early failure. Thus, Evidence 1 captures the commutative extraction of wealth, while Evidence 8 captures the systemic degradation of commerce.

Third, Circular Industrial Commons versus the Forms of Alienation (Döngüsel İktisat ile Akit Kalıpları Dengesi: Bey' ve Hizmet Modeli):
A foundational alignment exists between the prevention of planetary waste (ifsâd fi'l-arz) and commercial contract structures:
When an asset is conveyed under an absolute sale (bey'), full proprietary sovereignty (milk tamm) vests in B; M is completely stripped of unilateral post-sale control, remote impairment powers, and monopolistic repair barriers.
Conversely, where commercial engineering adopts genuine circular economy frameworks—such as Product-as-a-Service, functional leasing, or closed-loop stewardship (al-icâre ve istisnâ')—the economic incentive for planned obsolescence is organically eliminated: because the physical asset remains on M's balance sheet, every premature failure is a direct financial loss to M, turning maximal durability, modular repairability, and material recycling into M's primary profit drivers. What Islamic law condemns is neither genuine sale nor genuine lease/service, but the exploitative hybrid counterfeit: Big Tech's coercive pseudo-sale, wherein M extracts the capitalized, upfront acquisition price P of an outright sale, while surreptitiously retaining the unilateral remote authority of a perpetual feudal overlord, trapping B in artificial obsolescence.

Fourth, Pure Economic Loss versus Post-Sale Trespass (Ayb ile İtlâf ve Economic Loss Doktrini):
In tort law, the economic loss doctrine bars delictual recovery when a product merely disappoints commercial expectations or deteriorates through internal defect without harming persons or separate property. The model strictly honors this boundary:
In Model One (Factory Phase), premature termination is legally characterized not as a violent external trespass (itlâf), but as an upstream commutative deceit (al-taghrîr al-fi'lî) and intentional delivery of a defective benchmark asset, enforceable through contractual defect options (hıyâru'l-ayb, Mecelle Article 337) against vendor S, with statutory indemnification cascading up the commercial chain to M, and direct regulatory sanctions by the hisbah.
In Models Two and Three (Post-Sale Phase), M's liability is not barred by the economic loss doctrine because M commits an affirmative, post-conveyance external trespass (mubâşereten teaddî ve itlâf) upon already-vested external property across communication networks or internal logic triggers, and imposes an artificial monopolistic restraint upon external chattel.

Fifth, The Risk of Time versus Sabotage: Honoring Al-Kharâju bi'd-Damân (Zaman Riski ile Kasıtlı İtlaf Ayrımı):
Under the foundational Hadith and maxim codified in Mecelle Article 87 ("Al-kharâju bi'd-damân" / Revenue follows liability for loss) and Article 85 ("Al-gurmu bi'l-gunm" / Burden follows benefit), the buyer who reaps the operational yield of a durable asset after delivery (kabz) justly bears the natural temporal risks of physical wear, chemical entropy, and genuine technological depreciation (amortisman ve meşru kesâd). The manufacturer is never an open-ended insurer against time or entropy. The model does not shift post-delivery risk onto M: natural fatigue is strictly borne by B. What the law prohibits is not the natural wear borne by the owner, but M's affirmative, unannounced planting of artificial self-destruct mechanisms and post-sale remote degradation. B assumes the risk of nature; B never assumes the risk of hidden sabotage by the maker.

Sixth, Systemic Security Costs versus Disproportionate Impairment (Sıfır-Maliyetli Güvenlik Yanılsamasının Reddi):
In complex computing architectures, addressing profound microarchitectural vulnerabilities or evolving cryptographic standards can unavoidably impose an authentic physical computation penalty (e.g., architectural mitigation overhead). Islamic jurisprudence recognizes the reality of technical necessity (darûrah); however, under Mecelle Article 22, necessity is bounded by strict proportionality (al-darûrâtü tütekkaderu bi-kaderihâ). An authentic security mitigation that incidentally degrades performance within strictly necessary, mathematically demonstrable parameters to repel catastrophic data compromise is legally recognized; what is prohibited is seizing upon a minor vulnerability or normal wear to engineer massive, unannounced, and permanent functional paralysis that renders the asset useless to coerce repeat purchasing.

Planned obsolescence, as an actionable commercial wrong and systemic delict, exists wherever an artificial limit L, anti-repair lock, or ecosystem restraint is imposed without strictly proportionate technical necessity, truncating baseline utility and externalizing ecological damage onto society.
The procedural rule of adjudication is objective: B or the regulatory authority presents a prima facie showing of premature failure, statistical fleet anomaly, or artificial modular lockout. Evidentiary tools—neutral expert (ahl al-khibrah) teardowns, reverse engineering, and telemetry audits—shift the burden to M to demonstrate that no less restrictive, open alternative could preserve functionality.

By planned obsolescence I mean an affirmative design choice, architectural lockout, or post-conveyance intervention by the maker that artificially and disproportionately shortens the useful capacity of a good, or affirmatively extinguishes the owner's legal ability to maintain, recalibrate, and operate it. Three boundaries delineate legitimate commerce from unlawful destruction:
First, natural material wear, chemical aging, and entropy, which are borne by the owner under al-kharâju bi'd-damân.
Second, authentic computational exhaustion: where older hardware lacks the physical computational capacity to execute newly developed functions, M may cease feature enhancements, leaving legacy software intact in its stable working state.
Third, standalone retirement of third-party cloud infrastructure: where an external server infrastructure terminates with reasonable notice, provided M does not affirmatively brick the local hardware or deploy cryptographic locks to block autonomous operation.
Crucially, declaring a premature design limit openly on packaging does not sanitize the act: disclosure resolves bilateral deceit, but leaves the systemic ecological offense against the commons subject to regulatory invalidation and prohibition.

The talk has two sides. On the first side stand our evidences, eight of them, built on four models. On the second side stand the producer defenses, four of them, and I will refute each one.

MODEL ONE: THE LIMIT PLANTED BEFORE THE SALE.
Here is the model. M manufactures cyber-physical asset X. In manufacturing, M has two design configurations, A one and A two. A one grants X its authentic uncurtailed functional capacity and structural durability benchmark E inherent in its bill of materials and chassis design, allowing wear-bound auxiliary subcomponents to be non-destructively maintained, calibrated, or renewed. A two imposes an artificial structural, logical, or monolithic barrier that irreversibly couples the lifespan of the entire asset to the natural exhaustion of its fastest-degrading wear subcomponent, terminating X at an artificially truncated capacity ceiling L. Between A one and A two, any claimed technical or cost difference T is either nonexistent or grossly trivial and disproportionate to the massive destruction of operational utility (E - L). B is bound to M's ecosystem by switching barriers W. M chooses A two to accelerate replacement demand within its captive domain. At conveyance t zero, B observes the customary market benchmark E and pays price P corresponding to a sound, serviceable asset. For analytical clarity, let E represent ten units of baseline functional capacity, and let L represent an engineered ceiling of four units. P was paid upon the normative assumption of ten units of unhindered capacity.

OUR FIRST EVIDENCE: COMMUTATIVE DISPARITY, LATENT DEFECT, AND UPSTREAM FRAUD.
The claim: in commutative transactions, fair market valuation is determined by intrinsic operational capacity and fatigue resistance; introducing an artificial structural or monolithic bottleneck constitutes an actionable upstream latent defect (ayb) and fraudulent disparity (gabn-i fâhiş bi't-taghrîr), enforceable via contractual rescission against S and upstream restitutionary indemnification against M.
The proof: durable assets are acquired for their authentic capacity and unhindered stream of utility. When M manufactures asset X with engineered limitation L, M introduces an objective defect into the commercial stream of commerce. In the period of informational opacity, or under switching barrier W, price P is maintained at the valuation of sound benchmark capacity E. While contract law tolerates minor subjective valuation differences (gabn-i yesîr), neutral commercial appraisers (ahl al-khibrah) evaluate the true financial value of an asset whose intrinsic utility is structurally curtailed from ten units of capacity to four. Because an asset designed to force premature abandonment upon the failure of an unrenewable wear-component loses a major proportion of its commercial value at the very moment of exchange, the resulting gap between the price paid P and the true objective value constitutes an unconscionable disparity (gabn-i fâhiş) accompanied by active fraudulent concealment (taghrîr). Under the economic loss doctrine and classical sales jurisprudence, this upstream factory-phase flaw is strictly categorized as an original latent defect (ayb-i kadîm / ayb-i hafî)—not an external physical trespass (itlâf)—because the failure occurs purely within the internal commercial boundary of the asset itself.
The Qur'an commands in Surah An-Nisa, verse 29: "Do not consume your wealth among yourselves by false ways." Extracting the full market price of a durable, serviceable good while delivering an asset burdened with an engineered kill-condition is consuming property by falsehood (ekl-i emvâl bi'l-bâtıl).
The rule: against immediate seller S, under Mecelle Article 356, B possesses the contractual remedy of rescission (faskh): B may cancel the contract and recover purchase price P upon tendering restitution. Under Mecelle Article 358, where restitution of the physical good is barred due to alteration or consumption, contractual rescission strictly lapses. Crucially, intermediate seller S is an innocent retailer; once S compensates B or accepts rescission, S possesses the established right of upstream recourse (rücû') against manufacturer M to recover all disbursed sums. Furthermore, where M organized the deceptive distribution scheme, public commercial law and hisbah regulations compel M to directly disgorge the unearned price differential (noksan-ı semen).

OUR SECOND EVIDENCE: OBJECTIVE LATENT DEFECT AND CASCADE INDEMNIFICATION.
The claim: an engineered durability limit L constitutes an objective original latent defect (ayb-i hafî), entitling B to contractual defect remedies (khiyâr al-ayb) against S and cascade indemnification from M, without requiring proof of subjective internal malice.
The proof: durability in a durable cyber-physical asset is an implied core condition of fitness and merchantable quality within its chosen price tier. M alone holds the proprietary blueprints, telemetry, and source code containing limit L; neither intermediate seller S nor buyer B has the means of discovering it at t zero. The distribution of an artificially curtailed good into commerce creates an objective misrepresentation by conduct, warranting that the good conforms to sound custom and unadulterated baseline engineering. The Prophet, peace be upon him, forbade the transaction infected with gharar, reported by Muslim. He also stated: "Whoever deceives us is not one of us," reproaching the merchant who concealed wet grain beneath dry grain. Hidden limit L is the wet grain at the bottom of the vessel. The legal wrong is not M's failure to build an impossible ideal, but the covert embedding of an artificial failure bottleneck below the product's own physical component threshold.
The instance: B examines X at purchase and finds no visible flaw; the premature termination code, underspecified structural component, or physical assembly barrier resides in M's proprietary design.
The rule: under the governing law of latent defects (khiyâr al-ayb, Mecelle Article 337), B holds the fundamental option to return X to S and recover the full price P. Crucially, where restitution is rendered legally or physically impossible—such as where prolonged usage constitutes a legal bar to return (mâni'-i radd)—the action does not perish. Under Mecelle Articles 343, 344, and 345, B is entitled to sue directly for the pecuniary price diminution (noksan-ı semen / arsh). Under the rules of commercial subrogation, retail vendor S cascades this liability directly to manufacturer M. This preserves both the privity of contract and the economic loss rule: contractual ayb governs the internal failure of the good, while tortious itlâf is reserved for post-sale external trespasses.

OUR THIRD EVIDENCE: MANUFACTURED CAPTIVITY, UNLAWFUL ENRICHMENT, AND THE PREEMPTION OF BENEFIT.
The claim: no commercial entity may manufacture platform captivity to profit from the engineered destruction of its own products.
The proof: in a competitive, unencumbered market, an early failure would drive B to a competitor. But M constructs high switching costs W—locking B's data, peripheral accessories, and software ecosystem within M's walled garden. When M curtails X to L, M does not face competitive elimination; instead, M exploits B's captive state to force a repeat purchase. M's accelerated revenue is derived directly from B's artificial deprivation. A nominal engineering pretext T cannot sanitize this destruction: where the alleged technical saving is trivial while the loss of life is severe, the defense of necessity is defeated by the doctrine of proportionality (Mecelle Article 22). 
The legal principles are two. First, Mecelle Article 19: "Harm is eliminated" (Al-dararu yuzâl). Second, the fundamental maxim codified in Mecelle Article 99: "Men iste'cele şey'en kable evânihî ûkıbe bi-hırmânih" (Whoever hastens something before its due time by an illegitimate means is penalized with deprivation thereof). In strict liability, whether categorized as direct destruction (mubâşir, Mecelle Article 92) or causal inducement (mutasabbib, Mecelle Article 93), the objective reality is that M engineered the premature failure to harvest repeat revenue; the law deprives M of that ill-gotten benefit through mandatory restitution, restitutionary damages (arsh), and the disgorgement of unearned replacement profits.
The instance: at four units of time, X fails prematurely. Because B's accumulated digital assets are locked in M's platform (W), B is economically coerced to repurchase from M, accelerating M's revenue cycle at the direct expense of B's wealth.

MODEL TWO: THE ACT ON THE GOOD AFTER THE SALE.
The model: the transaction is finalized at t zero, and complete ownership and physical custody reside with B. No imminent threat to systemic integrity arises, or any claimed justification T is grossly disproportionate to the damage inflicted. X performs every warranted function. At a subsequent time t one, M utilizes a retained logical trigger, deferred microcode command, or external modification channel to unilaterally throttle performance, disable existing functional capacities, or induce artificial system rejection of compatible maintenance interventions.

OUR FOURTH EVIDENCE: UNLAWFUL TRESPASS, DIRECT DESTRUCTION, AND ABUSE OF CUSTODIAL ACCESS.
The claim: upon conveyance of ownership, M's proprietary authority over X is completely extinguished; any subsequent unilateral adverse modification constitutes an unlawful trespass and direct property destruction (mubâşereten itlâf).
The proof: sale transfers exclusive ownership (milk tamm) to B, encompassing the sole right to use, preserve, alter, recalibrate, or repair the cyber-physical asset. When M argues that B consented to background modifications via a standard-form electronic adhesion clause, contract law dictates that consent given for operational security and maintenance cannot be converted into a license to impair the good. In Islamic jurisprudence, custodial authority delegated for maintenance and preservation (amânat al-hifz) strictly excludes any act of impairment (itlâf); exceeding delegated custodial boundaries constitutes unauthorized disposal in another's property (tasarruf fî milk al-ghayr) and an actionable breach of trust. Under Mecelle Article 92, the direct actor (mubâşir) is strictly liable for property damage (itlâf) regardless of whether the act was executed with subjective malice or claimed administrative convenience.
The instance: at t one, X is functioning soundly. M deploys an unannounced firmware modification or triggers a deferred logic instruction. B discovers that operational throughput is curtailed or compatible replacement modules are arbitrarily rejected. M has directly invaded and impaired B's property.
The rule: M executed the adverse alteration directly through algorithmic intervention, acting as the direct tortfeasor (mubâshir). Under Mecelle Article 92, M is strictly liable for property damage. B is entitled to injunctive relief, specific performance—the full restoration of X to its warranted operating state—and compensatory damages for the incurred devaluation and lost utility.

MODEL THREE: THE CLOSING OF REPAIR.
The model: X suffers a natural fatigue or wear fault in an internal subcomponent. B, or an independent qualified technician, possesses the technical capability to restore or recalibrate it. The maintenance operation involves standard, compatible components or routine calibration procedures and does not compromise core security credentials, confidential user enclaves, or statutory safety standards, so any claimed technical excuse T is either nonexistent or grossly disproportionate. M withholds diagnostic interfaces and error specifications, imposes proprietary cryptographic or physical lockouts that artificially reject non-proprietary replacement components, or legally and logically shuts down independent diagnostic calibration utilities.

OUR FIFTH EVIDENCE: VOID RESTRAINTS, MANUFACTURED DISTRESS, AND MONOPOLISTIC IMPEDIMENTS.
The claim: when M conditions the operation of X upon an absolute prohibition of external maintenance, M imposes a void restraint that contradicts the essence of ownership, manufactures artificial scarcity, and exploits the owner's necessitous state.
The proof has three steps. 
First, an absolute conveyance inherently transfers the unrestricted right of functional maintenance, servicing, and recalibration to the owner. A restraint imposed by M—whether by contractual clause, artificial proprietary validation locks, or software calibration barriers—prohibiting the owner from accessing, servicing, or recalibrating his property directly contradicts the essence of sale (muqtadâ al-aqd). Under Islamic jurisprudence, a condition that strips the buyer of the essential prerogatives of ownership while securing an unearned monopoly for the maker is a corrupting condition (shart fâsid), rendering the restriction null and void ab initio. 
Second, when M bundles auxiliary component verification into the core security root of trust under the pretext of systemic integrity, M manufactures artificial scarcity (ihtikâr) in essential maintenance. Modern systems engineering and cryptography disprove M's false dilemma between security and repairability: systemic integrity is fully preserved through compartmentalized zero-knowledge verification and decoupled public authorization layers, which permit modular component renewal and logical re-calibration without exposing private master credentials, biometric enclaves, or network secrets. Conflating auxiliary maintenance with core credential exposure is an engineered evasion (sû-i isti'mâl-i hak). Imam Abu Yusuf broadened the doctrine of ihtikâr to encompass any commodity, service, or vital facility whose deliberate withholding inflicts public hardship. Contemporary jurists, reasoning by systematic extrapolation (takhrîj), extend this prohibition to essential replacement modules, diagnostic protocols, and calibration tools whose monopolistic withholding inflicts systemic hardship (haraj) on the public. 
Third, by locking all independent maintenance avenues, M forces the owner into a state of captive distress (iztirâr), compelling B to pay whatever extortionate fee M dictates or prematurely discard X. Abu Dawud reports from Ali, may God be pleased with him, that the Prophet forbade the exploitative transaction forced upon a person in distress (bay' al-mudtarr).
The instance: an unbonded physical interface, auxiliary input/output module, or wear-bound subcomponent degrades. M's proprietary lockout rejects an identical, fully certified replacement module or withholds the re-calibration protocol, forcing B to pay an extortionate fee at M's exclusive facility or discard X.
The rule: under Mecelle Article 100, M's attempt to restrict completed ownership is legally void. The restrictive lock is an unlawful impediment, and B has the untrammeled right to repair, recalibrate, and sustain his property through any competent provider.

MODEL FOUR: THE PLATFORM AND SECTOR DYNAMICS.
The model: Models One, Two and Three expand from isolated practices into an industry-wide platform oligopoly or cartel. In this market, manufacturers leverage high switching barriers W (structural lock-in, non-interoperable peripherals, closed interfaces, and cumulative capital dependencies) to extinguish consumer exit. The model analyzes two successive phases: the informational opacity phase where captive repeat purchases occur, followed by the revelation phase where collective resource squandering and public harm erupt.

OUR SIXTH EVIDENCE: SYSTEMIC SQUANDERING AND THE INALIENABLE COMMONS.
The claim: premature artificial obsolescence externalizes catastrophic ecological burdens onto the planetary commons (ifsâd fi'l-arz), a public harm (darar âmm) that cannot be validated or legalized by private buyer consent, nor justified under the guise of life-cycle optimization.
The proof: in an industrial metabolic cycle, truncating the operational capacity of an asset from E to L forces society to extract, refine, manufacture, and discard complex materials at an accelerated velocity. In environmental economics and Life-Cycle Assessment (LCA), the primary ecological violence of high-tech cyber-physical assets resides not merely in discarded mass, but in the irrecoverable thermodynamic, chemical, and mineral manufacturing debt incurred during initial fabrication. By prematurely ending the useful life of the composite asset at L due to a locked subcomponent, M externalizes uncompensated entropy and toxicity onto the biosphere while the initial thermodynamic investment remains unamortized. 
M cannot evade this liability by pointing to marginal operational energy improvements in newer models: under rigorous Life-Cycle Assessment, microscopic operational gains cannot offset the massive embodied ecological footprint and resource extraction required to fabricate an entirely new replacement asset. Accelerating asset turnover through anti-repair design systematically breaches the planetary carrying capacity, generating irreversible public corruption (ifsâd fi'l-arz).
Regarding causal liability: B is merely the innocent consumer trapped within M's locked ecosystem. M is the platform architect who engineered premature mortality. In tort, M's liability is objective: M introduces an engineered agent of destruction into the public stream of commerce.
The foundational maxims of the Mecelle govern this destruction: a private commercial interest is overridden to repel a general public harm (Article 26); the greater harm is removed by the lesser (Article 27); and the burden follows the benefit (Mecelle Article 85: "Al-gurmu bi'l-gunm"). In Islamic jurisprudence, private contractual autonomy (al-hurriyyah al-ta'âqudiyyah) is strictly bounded by public welfare (al-maslahah al-'âmmah). Just as two private parties cannot contract to poison a public river, an informed buyer and a manufacturer cannot contract to squander scarce resources and poison the biosphere. The ecological commons (hakk-ı âm) is inalienable.
The instance: a population of N users requires durable utility over a timeline of twelve units. When baseline capacity E is ten, replacement throughput is minimal; when L is curtailed to four within locked platforms, the volume of extracted, processed, and discarded assets multiplies, needlessly poisoning the earth.

OUR SEVENTH EVIDENCE: LEGISLATIVE WISDOM: ENMITY AND THE CORROSION OF TRUST.
The claim: the systemic practice of planned obsolescence breeds widespread social hostility and institutional cynicism once discovered, defeating the overarching legislative purpose of commercial ethics.
In juristic methodology, enmity is not the operative legal ground (illa) of the prohibition, because an illa must be an objective, measurable attribute. The operative grounds are objective constructive destruction (helâk-ı hükmî), unconscionable disparity, and tortious damage (itlâf). Enmity is the underlying legislative wisdom (hikma) of the prohibition. The Qur'an states in Surah Al-Ma'idah, verse 91: "Satan only seeks to sow enmity and hatred among you." The identical wisdom underpins the prohibition of commercial betrayal and platform entrapment.
The proof: when a cyber-physical device perishes of natural wear or unpreventable economic obsolescence, B harbors no grievance; he recognizes physical reality. But when the hidden restriction L, the artificial throttling routine, or the deliberate lockout of repair becomes manifest through any credible means, consumer perception transforms from ordinary trade into captive exploitation. The resulting friction erupts into collective litigation, consumer boycotts, and systemic market distrust.
The instance: the objective reality of the hidden limit L or the artificial throttling patch becomes publicly manifest. The revelation destroys goodwill, replacing market trust with defensive hostility.
The rule: the prohibition stands firmly upon its operative grounds of latent defect, constructive destruction, and delictual damage, and the eruption of public enmity confirms the legislative wisdom of the law.

OUR EIGHTH EVIDENCE: ADVERSE SELECTION AND THE CAPTIVITY OF HONEST TRADE.
The claim: across market timelines, the concealment of durability systematically drives honest producers out of the marketplace, while platform lock-in prevents the natural price deflation predicted by classic lemon theory.
The proof: in textbook economic theory, a market infected with hidden defects collapses into a "market for lemons" where consumer willingness-to-pay drops to the quality of the lemon. But in cyber-physical platform capitalism, two structural distortions prevent this benign self-correction:
First, dynamic temporal opacity: before the systemic reality of limit L is publicly exposed, buyers cannot distinguish between honest durable goods and engineered goods. Honest producer M1, who builds for baseline durability E, cannot command a fair longevity premium because M2 masks limit L behind identical exterior aesthetics and marketing. Uninformed buyers attribute premature failure to accidental misfortune or natural component decay, repeatedly purchasing from M2. M2 captures outsized replacement revenues and marketing dominance, systematically bankrupting M1.
Second, platform switching captivity (W): once consumers become aware that all market devices fail prematurely, classic theory predicts that price P should deflate to match truncated lifespan L. However, platform lock-in (proprietary data, cloud archives, linked accessories, and application ecosystems) paralyzes consumer exit. Because abandoning M2 entails devastating capital and data switching costs, M2 continues to extract premium price P for truncated lifespan L. The market never reaches an honest competitive price equilibrium; it degrades into a coercive tollbooth where consumers pay full price for shortened lifecycles.
The rule: harm must be eliminated (Mecelle Article 19), and private gain cannot be tolerated when it destroys market integrity (Mecelle Article 26). Here the procedural duality of Islamic law resolves the tension between micro-contract remedies and macro-market order: while individual buyers utilize contractual options (hıyârât) against retailers S and product liability claims against makers M, prolonged societal use and widespread adoption can never convert a predatory practice into binding law. Under the foundational maxims of Islamic jurisprudence, a corrupt custom (al-'urf al-fâsid) that conflicts with clear legal texts and general welfare carries zero normative authority—even if it achieves universal industry prevalence. Consequently, the public regulatory authority (hisbah and siyâsah shar'iyyah) bears the non-delegable duty to intervene ex officio: invalidating collusive industry conventions, enforcing statutory transparency and minimum durability benchmarks, and penalizing engineered obsolescence to preserve authentic commerce and public trust.

THE JURISTIC ACCOUNTING OF GROUNDS AND SYSTEMIC HARMS.
To preserve precise methodology, the grounds are categorized into operative legal grounds (illal) and objective rational harms (mafâsid):

Six operative legal grounds (illal) derive from the texts, contract law, and the law of delict:
1. Eating wealth unjustly (ekl-i emvâl bi'l-bâtıl), in Evidence 1.
2. Gross disparity between price and utility (gabn-i fâhiş), in Evidence 1.
3. Concealment and unannounced defects (taghrîr and ayb-i hafî), in Evidence 2.
4. Objective constructive destruction and tortious damage (helâk-ı hükmî ve itlâf, Mecelle Articles 92 and 93), in Evidences 1, 3 and 4.
5. Unauthorized disposal and abuse of custodial access (tasarruf fî milk al-ghayr ve teaddî), in Evidence 4.
6. The corrupting restraint, artificial withholding, and exploitation of captive distress (shart fâsid, ihtikâr, and bay' al-mudtarr), in Evidences 3 and 5.

Four objective rational and systemic harms (mafâsid) derive from economic facts and legal maxims:
7. Unlawful preemption and premature appropriation of benefit (isticel, Mecelle Article 99), in Evidence 3.
8. Systemic misallocation of productive capital, in Evidence 3.
9. Ecological waste and public harm to the commons (darar âmm / ifsâd fi'l-arz, Mecelle Article 26), in Evidence 6.
10. Systemic market collapse through adverse selection and platform lock-in, in Evidence 8.

Enmity (adâvet) remains the overarching legislative wisdom (hikma) and is not counted among the operative causes. 
Under the higher objectives of Islamic law (Maqâsid al-Sharî'ah), planned obsolescence violates the protection of property (hifz al-mâl), commutative justice, social peace, and environmental balance.

NOW THE DEFENSES OF THE PRODUCERS.

The first defense: "The buyer accepted the terms of sale and license agreements. Full disclosure and consent were given."
The refutation: this defense fails on two distinct juristic planes—the plane of private contract and the plane of the public commons.
First, in private contract law, consent must be free, informed, and directed toward a lawful subject matter. Modern manufacturers never disclose engineered mortality; their business model relies on concealing L while charging the price of E. Furthermore, adhesion clauses where B supposedly agrees that M may unilaterally degrade performance or brick functions remotely are void ab initio. In Islamic jurisprudence, a condition that negates the core objective of the contract (muqtadâ al-aqd) is a corrupting condition (shart fâsid). A standard-form adhesion clause cannot legalize an unlawful tort or property destruction.
Second, and most fundamentally: even if M placed an explicit notice on the packaging stating: "This device is engineered to terminate at four units of time and cannot be repaired," that disclosure merely cures bilateral deceit (taghrîr); it does not legalize the systemic destruction of the commons. Private consent cannot sanctify public corruption (ifsâd fi'l-arz) or the wanton generation of toxic e-waste (darar âmm). The public authority (hisbah) retains the peremptory obligation to outlaw engineered obsolescence regardless of private contractual assent.

The second defense: "We had no choice. For systemic safety, component fatigue mitigation, or statutory environmental compliance, we had to intervene and restrict the asset."
The refutation: this is the classic attempt to use a nominal technical factor T as an absolute immunity shield. But modern jurisprudence rejects the binary fallacy: T is governed by proportionality (al-tenâsüb) and the prohibition of abuse of rights (sû-i isti'mâl-i hak).
First, under Mecelle Article 22, "Necessities are measured by their true proportion" (Al-darûrâtü tütekkaderu bi-kaderihâ). When an engineering constraint emerges—such as an internal wear-bound subcomponent naturally exhausting its operational tolerance and threatening system stability, or updated statutory standards—a manufacturer cannot seize upon that localized subcomponent fatigue as an excuse to impose permanent, unannounced system-wide throttling that coerces the owner into discarding the enduring asset. 
Second, necessity does not extinguish liability: Mecelle Article 33 establishes the timeless maxim: "Al-idtirâru lâ yubtilu haqqa'l-ghayr" (Necessity does not invalidate the right of another). Where an authentic, unavoidable safety hazard emerges, M is duty-bound to protect safety, but M cannot inflict uncompensated, permanent property devaluation upon B. Under Islamic product liability principles, M remains strictly bound to remedy the underlying bottleneck: M must issue a transparent safety notification, enable modular subcomponent renewal or recalibration at cost or under warranty, and restore X to sound operating condition. Converting an internal wear-differential into an irreversible system slowdown that manufactures replacement demand is not safety engineering; it is an unlawful evasion (hile-i şer'iyye) and commercial deceit masquerading as necessity.

The third defense: "We sold the physical hardware, but we retained the software. We licensed the code, and modern connected devices are dynamic cyber-physical services, not static physical chattel."
The refutation: this defense correctly recognizes that modern goods are cyber-physical, but reaches a legally perverse conclusion. Software and connectivity must be analyzed across three distinct juristic categories:
First, independent application software acquired separately: this can be legitimately licensed under copyright terms.
Second, embedded firmware and operating systems essential to the hardware's operation: without this code, X is reduced to an inert piece of material substrate. In jurisprudence, the accessory follows the principal: Mecelle Article 47 dictates that "what is subordinate to a thing in existence is subordinate to it in its legal ruling" (Al-tâbi'u tâbiun), and Article 48 states that "the subordinate cannot be given an independent ruling contrary to the principal." The cyber-physical good is the principal subject of sale; the integrated firmware is its indispensable attribute. The sale of X inherently conveys the permanent right to the functional utility of that embedded code. M retains intellectual property copyrights, but M cannot revoke, throttle, or weaponize the operational capacity of the sold property.
Third, connected network services and ongoing maintenance channels: connected devices require security patches against evolving threats. Here, ongoing custodial access is legally categorized as a delegated trust (amânat al-hifz). M is empowered to deploy defensive security mitigations; where mitigating a catastrophic microarchitectural vulnerability unavoidably imposes a measurable computational overhead, that trade-off is recognized under strict necessity (darûrah) provided it satisfies strict proportionality (Mecelle Article 22). However, M is strictly forbidden from using this custodial channel to execute arbitrary, unannounced property impairments. Furthermore, the law does not demand that M maintain legacy cloud servers forever at its own expense—that would be an unearned positive servitude. Rather, the demand of justice is strictly negative: to cease active obstruction and remove artificial kill-switches. When M legitimately ends cloud support, M must not affirmatively brick the hardware or deploy anti-repair locks to block independent operation. M must simply step aside: implementing compartmentalized cryptographic verification and decoupled public authorization layers that allow the owner or independent technicians to authenticate replacement modules, execute calibrations, and sustain the asset autonomously without exposing root security credentials. 
Moreover, regarding contractual forms and circular sustainability: Islamic jurisprudence honors both absolute sales (bey') and authentic operational service contracts (al-icâre / Product-as-a-Service). What circular industrial ecology proves is that genuine service models—where the physical asset remains on M's balance sheet—naturally align profits with maximal asset longevity and zero-waste recovery. What Islamic law condemns as unlawful exploitation (tahakküm ve gabn) is the fraudulent counterfeit: where M charges the full capitalized purchase price P of an outright sale, but covertly retains the remote powers of a landlord, denying the buyer both the prerogatives of ownership and the maintenance warranties of a true lessor.

The fourth defense: "Planned obsolescence finances innovation. Accelerated replacement cycles generate the revenue required for research and development, lowering hedonic unit costs and driving rapid technological progress."
The refutation: this defense fails on contractual, ethical, and economic grounds.
First, put it into the model. M secretly curtails X to L, withholding this from B, and justifies the premature replacement sale as financing the next generation of goods. B paid for E and received L. The value of the unreceived life was expropriated for an unagreed research fund. A noble end cannot sanctify an unlawful means (al-ghâyatu lâ tubarriru'l-wasîlah). If B were willing to fund future research through premature obsolescence, M would disclose it openly; M's concealment is proof that B would reject the bargain.
Second, hedonic price deflation—the real drop in computing and manufacturing cost per unit of operational performance along exponential efficiency growth curves—is an authentic triumph of engineering, but it cannot justify artificial obsolescence. If the next-generation good is genuinely superior, consumers have an authentic incentive to upgrade voluntarily, without their existing property being artificially sabotaged. Hedonic progress thrives on voluntary adoption of better technology, not the forced destruction of existing utility. If older hardware cannot support the demanding computational requirements of newer software, M is fully entitled to freeze feature updates for that hardware, clearly announcing the end of support. Freezing feature enhancements preserves existing functionality without destroying the device. Genuine innovation elevates the new; planned obsolescence murders the old.

CONCLUSION.
The legal, rational, and moral ruling on planned obsolescence is definitively established wherever an artificial limit L or ecosystem lock-in is imposed without proportional necessity, or where a nominal technical pretext T is deployed to engineer unearned replacement sales:
First: to engineer premature failure L into a durable good by structurally preventing modular renewal is an act of upstream commutative deceit (taghrîr), latent defect (ayb), and actionable commercial fraud within the supply chain.
Second: to remotely degrade an asset after conveyance, or to deploy locks that paralyze independent maintenance and calibration, is an unlawful post-sale trespass (mubâşereten teaddî), an abuse of custodial trust, and a nullification of ownership.
Third: to force durable assets prematurely onto waste heaps generates severe, inalienable public harm (ifsâd fi'l-arz / darar âmm) that cannot be validated or sanitized by private contractual consent.
Fourth: to cloak these practices under the labels of license, safety, ecosystem security, or innovation does not diminish the wrong; it merely layers legal evasion upon public destruction.

For manufacturers and platform architects, the boundary is clear and just:
If your hardware reaches its technical limit, declare the end of updates transparently, freeze the software in a stable state, and decouple authorization layers to preserve local autonomy.
If an authentic safety hazard or subcomponent fatigue arises, intervene openly, issue a notice, and enable modular renewal or repair without coercive downclocking under the doctrine of proportionality.
If systemic security requires cryptographic validation, employ compartmentalized verification rather than proprietary monopoly locks that paralyze modular maintenance.
Transactions must be honest in form and substance: if conveyed through sale, proprietary control must be fully relinquished; if structured as circular service (icâre), the maker must bear full operational maintenance and life-cycle stewardship. Any commercial scheme that extracts the price of absolute sale while retaining the unilateral power to extinguish the good is not commerce; it is an unlawful commercial tyranny. Thank you.
