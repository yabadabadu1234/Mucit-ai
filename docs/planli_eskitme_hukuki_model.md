THE ROOT CHARACTERIZATION: THE SIX JURISTIC HARMONIES.
Before examining the models, the legal and economic reality of planned obsolescence must be freed from six critical misconceptions:

First, The Normative Definition of Defect versus Natural Fatigue Gradient (Ayıp ile Doğal Mukavemet Gradyanı Ayrımı):
In reliability engineering and materials science, a complex manufactured asset may incorporate constituent elements possessing divergent rates of natural degradation and physical entropy, or it may constitute an integrated monolithic architecture. Classical jurisprudence recognizes that the measure of defect (ayb) in an asset is not determined by comparing the lifespan of a faster-wearing element against more enduring elements (which would constitute kıyâs-ı maalfârık). Rather, the actionable wrong and latent defect (ayb-i hafî / taghrîr) resides strictly in the deliberate curtailment of intrinsic potential without legitimate proportional justification: whether Sâni intentionally designs the asset as an unserviceable monolithic block to prevent renewal of an exhausted element, deploys artificial functional barriers, or structurally implants a latent premature failure threshold into the asset's chemical, atomic, or logical constitution. The wrong is not natural wear, but the affirmative structural or logical suppression of the asset's inherent operational capacity.

Second, Resolving the Lemons Paradox: Dynamic Opacity versus Market Equilibrium (Limon Piyasası ile Gabn Geriliminin Çözümü):
An apparent tension arises between Evidence 1 (Mâlik pays full price Semen expecting durability AsliKapasite) and Evidence 8 (the market degrades into a lemon equilibrium where all goods fail at KasıtlıKısıt and prices adjust). This tension dissolves once the market is understood dynamically rather than statically:
At the micro-contract level (Evidence 1), transactions occur under informational opacity (taghrîr) or within platform-locked ecosystems (EsaretKatsayısı), where Sâni extracts a price Semen reflecting customary durability AsliKapasite while secretly delivering KasıtlıKısıt.
At the macro-market level (Evidence 8), the lemons dynamic operates as a destructive temporal vector: prolonged deceit systematically drives durable producers out of the market until honesty becomes economically impossible. In platform oligopolies, moreover, switching costs EsaretKatsayısı permanently sever price Semen from competitive discipline, allowing Sâni to charge premium prices for truncated lifespans even when consumers suspect early failure. Thus, Evidence 1 captures the commutative extraction of wealth, while Evidence 8 captures the systemic degradation of commerce.

Third, Circular Industrial Commons versus the Forms of Alienation (Döngüsel İktisat ile Akit Kalıpları Dengesi: Bey' ve Hizmet Modeli):
A foundational alignment exists between the prevention of planetary waste (ifsâd fi'l-arz) and commercial contract structures:
When an asset is conveyed under an absolute sale (bey'), full proprietary sovereignty (milk tamm) vests in Mâlik; Sâni is completely stripped of unilateral post-sale control, remote impairment powers, and monopolistic repair barriers.
Conversely, where commercial engineering adopts genuine circular economy frameworks—such as Product-as-a-Service, functional leasing, or closed-loop stewardship (al-icâre ve istisnâ')—the economic incentive for planned obsolescence is organically eliminated: because the physical asset remains on Sâni'nin balance sheet, every premature failure is a direct financial loss to Sâni, turning maximal durability, modular repairability, and material recycling into Sâni'nin primary profit drivers. What Islamic law condemns is neither genuine sale nor genuine lease/service, but the exploitative hybrid counterfeit: the coercive pseudo-sale of captive platform regimes, wherein Sâni extracts the capitalized, upfront acquisition price Semen of an outright sale, while surreptitiously retaining the unilateral remote authority of a perpetual feudal overlord, trapping Mâlik in artificial obsolescence.

Fourth, Pure Economic Loss versus Post-Sale Trespass (Ayb ile İtlâf ve Economic Loss Doktrini):
In tort law, the economic loss doctrine bars delictual recovery when a product merely disappoints commercial expectations or deteriorates through internal defect without harming persons or separate property. The model strictly honors this boundary:
In Model One (Factory Phase), premature termination is legally characterized not as a violent external trespass (itlâf), but as an upstream commutative deceit (al-taghrîr al-fi'lî) and intentional delivery of a defective benchmark asset, enforceable through contractual defect options (hıyâru'l-ayb, Mecelle Article 337) against vendor Bâi, with statutory indemnification cascading up the commercial chain to Sâni, and direct regulatory sanctions by the hisbah.
In Models Two and Three (Post-Sale Phase), Sâni'nin liability is not barred by the economic loss doctrine because Sâni commits an affirmative, post-conveyance external trespass (mubâşereten teaddî ve itlâf) upon already-vested external property across communication networks or internal logic triggers, and imposes an artificial monopolistic restraint upon external chattel.

Fifth, The Risk of Time versus Sabotage: Honoring Al-Kharâju bi'd-Damân (Zaman Riski ile Kasıtlı İtlaf Ayrımı):
Under the foundational Hadith and maxim codified in Mecelle Article 87 ("Al-kharâju bi'd-damân" / Revenue follows liability for loss) and Article 85 ("Al-gurmu bi'l-gunm" / Burden follows benefit), the buyer who reaps the operational yield of a durable asset after delivery (kabz) justly bears the natural temporal risks of physical wear, chemical entropy, and genuine technological depreciation (amortisman ve meşru kesâd). The manufacturer is never an open-ended insurer against time or entropy. The model does not shift post-delivery risk onto Sâni: natural fatigue is strictly borne by Mâlik. What the law prohibits is not the natural wear borne by the owner, but Sâni'nin affirmative, unannounced planting of artificial self-destruct mechanisms and post-sale remote degradation. Mâlik assumes the risk of nature; Mâlik never assumes the risk of hidden sabotage by the maker.

Sixth, Systemic Security Costs versus Disproportionate Impairment (Sıfır-Maliyetli Güvenlik Yanılsamasının Reddi):
In complex asset architectures, addressing profound structural security vulnerabilities or evolving integrity standards can unavoidably impose an authentic physical operational penalty (e.g., integrity mitigation overhead). Islamic jurisprudence recognizes the reality of technical necessity (darûrah); however, under Mecelle Article 22, necessity is bounded by strict proportionality (al-darûrâtü tütekkaderu bi-kaderihâ). An authentic security mitigation that incidentally degrades performance within strictly necessary, mathematically demonstrable parameters to repel catastrophic operational failure or compromise is legally recognized; what is prohibited is seizing upon a minor vulnerability or normal wear to engineer massive, unannounced, and permanent functional paralysis that renders the asset useless to coerce repeat purchasing.

Planned obsolescence, as an actionable commercial wrong and systemic delict, exists wherever an artificial limit KasıtlıKısıt, anti-repair lock, or ecosystem restraint is imposed without strictly proportionate technical necessity, truncating baseline utility and externalizing ecological damage onto society.
The procedural rule of adjudication is objective: Mâlik or the regulatory authority presents a prima facie showing of premature failure, statistical fleet anomaly, or artificial modular lockout. Evidentiary tools—neutral expert (ahl al-khibrah) teardowns, reverse engineering, and operational log audits—shift the burden to Sâni to demonstrate that no less restrictive, open alternative could preserve functionality.

By planned obsolescence I mean an affirmative design choice, architectural lockout, or post-conveyance intervention by the maker that artificially and disproportionately shortens the useful capacity of an asset, or affirmatively extinguishes the owner's legal ability to maintain, recalibrate, and operate it. Three boundaries delineate legitimate commerce from unlawful destruction:
First, natural material wear, chemical aging, and physical entropy, which are borne by the owner under al-kharâju bi'd-damân.
Second, authentic computational exhaustion: where an aging asset lacks the physical or structural capacity to execute newly developed functions, Sâni may cease feature enhancements, leaving existing operating routines intact in their stable working state.
Third, legitimate retirement of external continuous replenishment or service channels: where an ancillary external feed terminates with reasonable notice, provided Sâni does not affirmatively brick the local asset or deploy lockouts to block autonomous, standalone local operation.
Crucially, declaring a premature structural limit openly in standard contract terms or labeling does not sanitize the act: overt disclosure resolves bilateral deceit (taghrîr), but leaves the systemic ecological offense against the commons subject to regulatory invalidation and prohibition.

OUR FIRST EVIDENCE: COMMUTATIVE DISPARITY, LATENT DEFECT, AND UPSTREAM FRAUD.
The claim: in commutative transactions, fair market valuation is determined by intrinsic operational capacity and fatigue resistance; introducing an artificial structural or non-functional bottleneck constitutes an actionable upstream latent defect (ayb) and fraudulent disparity (gabn-i fâhiş bi't-taghrîr), enforceable via contractual rescission against Bâi and upstream restitutionary indemnification against Sâni.
The proof: durable assets are acquired for their authentic capacity and unhindered stream of utility. When Sâni manufactures asset Metâ with engineered limitation KasıtlıKısıt, Sâni introduces an objective defect into the commercial stream of commerce. In the period of informational opacity, or under switching barrier EsaretKatsayısı, price Semen is maintained at the valuation of sound benchmark capacity AsliKapasite. While contract law tolerates minor subjective valuation differences (gabn-i yesîr), neutral commercial appraisers (ahl al-khibrah) evaluate the true financial value of an asset whose intrinsic utility is structurally curtailed from ten units of capacity to four. Because an asset designed to force premature abandonment through artificial non-functional impediments loses a major proportion of its commercial value at the very moment of exchange, the resulting gap between the price paid Semen and the true objective value constitutes an unconscionable disparity (gabn-i fâhiş) accompanied by active fraudulent concealment (taghrîr). Under the economic loss doctrine and classical sales jurisprudence, this upstream factory-phase flaw is strictly categorized as an original latent defect (ayb-i kadîm / ayb-i hafî)—not an external physical trespass (itlâf)—because the failure occurs purely within the internal commercial boundary of the asset itself.
The Qur'an commands in Surah An-Nisa, verse 29: "Do not consume your wealth among yourselves by false ways." Extracting the full market price of a durable, serviceable good while delivering an asset burdened with an engineered kill-condition is consuming property by falsehood (ekl-i emvâl bi'l-bâtıl).
The rule: against immediate seller Bâi, under Mecelle Article 356, Mâlik possesses the contractual remedy of rescission (faskh): Mâlik may cancel the contract and recover purchase price Semen upon tendering restitution. Under Mecelle Article 358, where restitution of the physical good is barred due to alteration or consumption, contractual rescission strictly lapses. Crucially, intermediate seller Bâi is an innocent retailer; once Bâi compensates Mâlik or accepts rescission, Bâi possesses the established right of upstream recourse (rücû') against manufacturer Sâni to recover all disbursed sums. Furthermore, where Sâni organized the deceptive distribution scheme, public commercial law and hisbah regulations compel Sâni to directly disgorge the unearned price differential (noksan-ı semen).

OUR SECOND EVIDENCE: OBJECTIVE LATENT DEFECT AND CASCADE INDEMNIFICATION.
The claim: an engineered durability limit KasıtlıKısıt constitutes an objective original latent defect (ayb-i hafî), entitling Mâlik to contractual defect remedies (khiyâr al-ayb) against Bâi and cascade indemnification from Sâni, without requiring proof of subjective internal malice.
The proof: durability in a durable asset is an implied core condition of fitness and merchantable quality within its chosen price tier. Sâni alone holds the proprietary design specifications, operational logic, and architecture containing limit KasıtlıKısıt; neither intermediate seller Bâi nor buyer Mâlik has the means of discovering it at t0. The distribution of an artificially curtailed good into commerce creates an objective misrepresentation by conduct, warranting that the good conforms to sound custom and unadulterated baseline engineering. The Prophet, peace be upon him, forbade the transaction infected with gharar, reported by Muslim. He also stated: "Whoever deceives us is not one of us," establishing the universal prohibition against active commercial concealment (taghrîr). Limit KasıtlıKısıt is the covert concealment of an internal defect beneath an outwardly sound presentation. The legal wrong is not Sâni'nin failure to build an impossible ideal, but the covert embedding of an artificial failure bottleneck below the asset's own physical and structural threshold.
Model Proof: Mâlik examines Metâ at conveyance t0 and observes standard baseline durability AsliKapasite. The engineered limit KasıtlıKısıt (KasıtlıKısıt < AsliKapasite) is withheld within Sâni'nin asymmetric design specification, constituting latent defect (ayb-i hafî).
The rule: under the governing law of latent defects (khiyâr al-ayb, Mecelle Article 337), Mâlik holds the fundamental option to return Metâ to Bâi and recover the full price Semen. Crucially, where restitution is rendered legally or physically impossible—such as where prolonged usage constitutes a legal bar to return (mâni'-i radd)—the action does not perish. This preserves both the privity of contract and the economic loss rule: contractual ayb governs the internal failure of the good, while tortious itlâf is reserved for post-sale external trespasses.

The claim: no commercial entity may manufacture platform captivity to profit from the engineered destruction of its own products.

But Sâni constructs high switching barriers EsaretKatsayısı—locking Mâlik'in data, peripheral assets, and cumulative capital investments within Sâni'nin closed ecosystem. When Sâni curtails Metâ to KasıtlıKısıt, Sâni does not face competitive elimination; instead, Sâni exploits Mâlik'in captive state to force a repeat purchase. Sâni'nin accelerated revenue is derived directly from Mâlik'in artificial deprivation. A nominal engineering pretext TeknikZaruret cannot sanitize this destruction: where the alleged technical saving is trivial while the loss of life is severe, the defense of necessity is defeated by the doctrine of proportionality (Mecelle Article 22). 
The legal principles are two. First, Mecelle Article 19: "Harm is eliminated" (Al-dararu yuzâl). The first defense: "The buyer accepted the terms of sale and license agreements. Full disclosure and consent were given."
The refutation: this defense fails on two distinct juristic planes—the plane of private contract and the plane of the public commons.

Second, and most fundamentally: even if Sâni placed an explicit notice in standard terms or labeling stating: "This asset is engineered to terminate at four units of operational capacity and cannot be repaired," that disclosure merely cures bilateral deceit (taghrîr); it does not legalize the systemic destruction of the commons. The second defense: "We had no choice. For systemic safety, component fatigue mitigation, or statutory environmental compliance, we had to intervene and restrict the asset."
The refutation: this is the classic attempt to use a nominal technical factor TeknikZaruret as an absolute immunity shield. But modern jurisprudence rejects the binary fallacy: TeknikZaruret is governed by proportionality (al-tenâsüb) and the prohibition of abuse of rights (sû-i isti'mâl-i hak).

When an internal component wear-differential emerges (AltBileşenÖmrü << MetâGövdeÖmrü), a manufacturer cannot seize upon that localized exhaustion as an excuse to impose permanent, unannounced system-wide throttling that coerces the owner into discarding the enduring asset (Orantı = TeknikFayda / KayıpKapasite sıfıra yaklaşır).
Second, necessity does not extinguish liability: Mecelle Article 33 establishes the timeless maxim: "Al-idtirâru lâ yubtilu haqqa'l-ghayr" (Necessity does not invalidate the right of another). Where an authentic, unavoidable safety hazard emerges, Sâni is duty-bound to protect safety, but Sâni cannot inflict uncompensated, permanent property devaluation upon Mâlik. Under product liability principles, Sâni remains strictly bound to remedy the underlying bottleneck: Sâni must issue a transparent safety notification, preserve non-destructive component renewal or recalibration at cost or under warranty, and restore Metâ to sound operating condition. Converting an internal wear-differential into an irreversible system slowdown that manufactures replacement demand is not safety engineering; it is an unlawful evasion (hile-i şer'iyye) and commercial deceit masquerading as necessity.

The third defense: "We sold the physical substrate, but we retained the intellectual property in the governing logic. We licensed the code, and modern connected composite assets are dynamic continuous services, not static chattel."

Mathematical & Jurisprudential Specification:
* İstihlâk Eşiği (Exhaustion Boundary):
  Satış sonrası tüm anlarda (t > t0): Sâni Tasarruf Yetkisi ile Satılan Münferit Metâ kesişimi boş kümedir.
  Sâni Fikrî Hakkı: Sadece küllî çoğaltma ve ticari korsanlığı engellemeyi kapsar (Küllî Tasarruf).
  Mâlik İlk Satış Hakkı: Satın alınan münferit nüsha üzerinde mutlak çalıştırma, idâme, tamir ve yerel tadil hakkını bahşeder (Cüz'î Tasarruf).
Governing operational logic and ongoing service channels must therefore be categorized across three distinct juristic planes:
First, independent auxiliary routines acquired separately: this can be legitimately licensed under copyright terms as an autonomous, unbundled intangible asset.
Second, embedded functional logic indispensable to the asset's operation: without this code, Metâ is reduced to an inert piece of material substrate. Under the doctrine of exhaustion (istihlâk-ı hak), when Sâni sells Metâ for full purchase price Semen, Sâni'nin exclusive right to restrict the local use, maintenance, and execution of that specific embodied instance of logic is completely exhausted. Sâni retains the exclusive negative right against commercial unauthorized copying or public distribution of the code (preventing Mâlik or third parties from cloning and pirating Sâni'nin proprietary logic design). But Sâni cannot convert an intellectual property right into a perpetual negative easement or remote demolition authority over the conveyed chattel. To argue that retaining copyright justifies remote throttling or blocking repair tools is an actionable abuse of right (sû-i isti'mâl-i hak) and a void restraint (shart fâsid, Mecelle Article 100).
Third, connected network services and ongoing maintenance channels: connected composite assets require security updates against evolving threats. Here, ongoing custodial access is legally categorized as a delegated trust (amânat al-hifz). Sâni is empowered to deploy defensive security mitigations; where mitigating a catastrophic systemic vulnerability unavoidably imposes a measurable operational overhead, that trade-off is recognized under strict necessity (darûrah) provided it satisfies strict proportionality (Mecelle Madde 22: Orantı = TeknikKazanç / KapasiteKaybı > 0). However, Sâni is strictly forbidden from using this custodial channel to execute arbitrary, unannounced property impairments. 
Furthermore, justice does not demand theoretical architectural impossibilities; nor does it demand that Sâni maintain external host infrastructure forever at its own expense. The legal mandate is strictly negative and structural:
1. Negative Duty: Sâni must cease affirmative cryptographic serialization locks designed solely to block standard compatible replacement parts where no authentic safety root is exposed.
2. Architecture of Release: When Sâni legitimately terminates continuous service feeds, Sâni must not leave the asset permanently paralyzed; Sâni must release diagnostic authorization or open local maintenance interfaces, restoring full standalone autonomy to Mâlik.
Moreover, regarding contractual forms and circular sustainability: Islamic jurisprudence honors both absolute sales (bey') and authentic operational service contracts (al-icâre / Product-as-a-Service). What circular industrial ecology proves is that genuine service models—where the physical asset remains on Sâni'nin balance sheet—naturally align profits with maximal asset longevity and zero-waste recovery. What Islamic law condemns as unlawful exploitation (tahakküm ve gabn) is the fraudulent counterfeit: where Sâni charges the full capitalized purchase price Semen of an outright sale, but covertly retains the remote powers of a landlord, denying the buyer both the prerogatives of ownership and the maintenance warranties of a true lessor.

The fourth defense: "Planned obsolescence finances innovation. Accelerated replacement cycles generate the revenue required for research and development, lowering hedonic unit costs and driving rapid technological progress."
The refutation: this defense fails on contractual, ethical, and economic grounds.
First, put it into the model. Sâni secretly curtails Metâ to KasıtlıKısıt, withholding this from Mâlik, and justifies the premature replacement sale as financing the next generation of goods. Mâlik paid for AsliKapasite and received KasıtlıKısıt. The value of the unreceived life was expropriated for an unagreed research fund. A noble end cannot sanctify an unlawful means (al-ghâyatu lâ tubarriru'l-wasîlah). If Mâlik were willing to fund future research through premature obsolescence, Sâni would disclose it openly; Sâni'nin concealment is proof that Mâlik would reject the bargain.
Second, hedonic price deflation—the real decline in manufacturing and production cost per unit of operational output along technological efficiency curves—is an authentic triumph of human industry, but it cannot justify artificial obsolescence. If the next-generation good is genuinely superior, consumers have an authentic incentive to upgrade voluntarily, without their existing property being artificially sabotaged. Genuine progress thrives on voluntary adoption of superior utility, not the forced destruction of existing value. If an older asset substrate cannot support the demanding requirements of newer operational routines, Sâni is fully entitled to cease feature expansions or transition the asset to a stable, unchanging operational baseline, clearly announcing the end of lifecycle expansion. Preserving a stable baseline maintains existing functional integrity without destroying the asset. Genuine innovation elevates the production frontier through superior utility; planned obsolescence artificially extinguishes existing functional utility to force replacement.

CONCLUSION.
The legal, rational, and moral ruling on planned obsolescence is definitively established wherever an artificial limit KasıtlıKısıt or ecosystem lock-in is imposed without proportional necessity, or where a nominal technical pretext TeknikZaruret is deployed to engineer unearned replacement sales:
First: to engineer premature failure KasıtlıKısıt into a durable good by deploying non-functional structural barriers against renewal or embedding unannounced latent kill-conditions is an act of upstream commutative deceit (taghrîr), latent defect (ayb), and actionable commercial fraud within the supply chain.
Second: to remotely degrade an asset after conveyance, or to deploy locks that paralyze independent maintenance and calibration, is an unlawful post-sale trespass (mubâşereten teaddî), an abuse of custodial trust, and a nullification of ownership.
Third: to force durable assets prematurely past their designed capacity threshold [Toplam(AsliKapasite - KasıtlıKısıt) > MüştereklerEşiği] externalizes irrecoverable thermodynamic entropy onto the planetary commons (ifsâd fi'l-arz / darar âmm) that invalidates exculpatory contractual waivers without diminishing the buyer's vested ownership, triggering peremptory regulatory sanctions and restitution against the maker.
Fourth: to cloak these practices under the labels of license, safety, ecosystem security, or innovation does not diminish the wrong; it merely layers legal evasion upon public destruction.

For manufacturers and platform architects, the boundary is clear and just:
If your asset reaches its physical or computational capacity limit, declare the end of expansion transparently, maintain the operating regime in a stable state, and decouple authorization layers to preserve local autonomy.
If an authentic safety hazard or localized material fatigue arises, intervene openly, issue a notice, and enable modular renewal or repair without coercive functional degradation under the doctrine of proportionality (Mecelle Article 22).
If systemic security requires cryptographic validation, employ compartmentalized verification rather than proprietary monopoly locks that paralyze modular maintenance.
Transactions must be honest in form and substance: if conveyed through sale, proprietary control must be fully relinquished; if structured as circular service (icâre), the maker must bear full operational maintenance and life-cycle stewardship. Any commercial scheme that extracts the price of absolute sale while retaining the unilateral power to extinguish the good is not commerce; it is an unlawful commercial tyranny. Thank you.
